Results

All numbers come from the SQLite database in this repository. Each condition was simulated once.

1. First gait: fixed versus adaptive

The hand-chosen gait fell in all six runs. The gravity-scaled frequency only delayed the fall.

The body moved backwards (negative distance) in every first-gait run, so a longer survival time was not a gain in walking.

2. Tuned gait

After a separate 13-parameter search for each controller, all six runs stayed upright for 12 s.

The adaptive controller kept the body nearly upright (largest pitch 3.1° to 4.6°). The fixed controller leaned forward by about 5° to 7° and moved farther on Earth and the Moon. On Mars the adaptive controller advanced farther. There is no single winner.

3. Before and after tuning

Tuning raised the survival time from 1.7 to 3.2 s to the full 12 s. That change is about ten times larger than the 11% to 13% gained from the frequency law.

4. Standing tests

Neutral-pose standing for 10 s in each environment, and three foot widths with no disturbance. Differences are tiny and without practical meaning.

Foot width (7, 9 and 11 cm)

What the results do not show

  • One run per condition, so there is no estimate of spread.
  • The simulated motors can give 2.0 N·m, about twice what an MG996R servo can give at best.
  • The controller reads exact pitch values, while a real one would use a filtered sensor estimate.
  • Several tuned values lie near the edge of the search range, so better settings may exist.
